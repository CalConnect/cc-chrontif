
[[principles]]
== Principles

=== General

The requirements of this document are derived from a set of design principles.
Each principle distinguishes the trust infrastructure specified here from
traditional public key infrastructure (PKI) patterns that were designed for
server-identity authentication and are structurally inadequate for establishing
the trustworthiness of persistent artifacts.

NOTE: This clause is motivational. The binding normative requirements derived
from these principles are specified in clauses 8 through 20. In case of any
apparent conflict, the normative clauses take precedence.

The name CHRONTIF encodes seven architectural properties. These properties are
not merely descriptive — each maps to normative requirements in this document:

.CHRONTIF architectural properties by letter
[cols="1,2,4",options="header"]
|===
| Letter | Property | Architectural meaning

| C
| Convergent
| Multiple independent attestation paths converge into one verifiable record.
Each {{trust dimension}} (data, person, time, location, environment, and others
defined by the scheme) produces an independent {{co-signature}} on the same
{{canonical payload}}. Convergence, not single-signer authority, establishes
trustworthiness. See <<artifact-multi-dimensional>>.

| H
| Hierarchical
| Signing authority flows through a delegation hierarchy — {{root trust
authority}} through {{delegated trust authority,delegated trust authorities}}
to {{end certificate}} to {{trusted artifact}} — with {{scope}} narrowing
monotonically at every link. See <<architecture-authorities>> and
<<scope-monotonic-narrowing>>.

| R
| Repudiable
| Trust is a lifecycle, not a permanent state. Previously-granted trust can be
formally revoked through {{trust repudiation}} — threshold-gated revocation
mechanisms that propagate to all artifacts transitively bound to the revoked
state. This introduces a trust lifecycle concept not present in traditional PKI's certificate-validity model: trust granted under conditions can be withdrawn when those conditions cease to hold. See
<<revocation-propagation>>.

| O
| Open
| Verification requires no phone-home to the issuer. The {{trust anchor
bundle}} enables fully offline verification. Transparency logs are publicly
auditable. No proprietary component is required to verify a CHRONTIF artifact.
See <<architecture-anchors>> and <<transparency>>.

| N
| Network
| Authority is exercised through threshold groups and federated trust
authorities that span organizational boundaries. The {{trust graph}} is a
directed acyclic graph, not a linear chain. No single entity controls any
level of the hierarchy. See <<threshold-signing>> and <<threshold-federated>>.

| T
| Trust
| The domain of this document is trust: the establishment, assessment, and
withdrawal of confidence in signed artifacts. Trust is graduated (via the
{{classification label}}), not binary. See <<verification-classification>>.

| I, F
| Infrastructure, Framework
| CHRONTIF is an infrastructure-level framework, not a single product or
protocol. It defines requirements that independent implementations conform to,
and that domain-specific profiles instantiate. See <<conformance>>.
|===

The principles are grouped thematically below. Each principle appears in at
least one requirements class defined in <<conformance>>.

NOTE: These principles distinguish CHRONTIF from traditional PKI patterns
optimized for server-identity authentication. They address the persistent-
artifact trust problem specifically; they are not criticisms of PKI, which
remains the appropriate and mature technology for TLS, code signing under
active policy enforcement, and identity binding. See <<annex-b>> for
use-case suitability guidance.


=== Authority and delegation

==== Cryptographic scope enforcement

The {{scope}} of a {{trust authority}} shall be a first-class signed field,
cryptographically enforced at every chain link. Widening any {{scope dimension}}
at any delegation point shall cause a hard verification failure. This contrasts with
traditional PKI, in which a CA's scope is defined by external policy
(Baseline Requirements) and is not verifiable from the certificate itself.

==== Threshold at every level

A {{quorum}} of cooperating key holders shall be required to exercise the
signing authority of any {{trust authority}}, including but not limited to the
{{root trust authority}}. There shall be no single point of cryptographic
compromise at any level of the {{trust graph}}.

==== Federated trust authorities

A {{federated trust authority}} shall be a threshold group of independent
organizations sharing a single {{aggregate key}}, such that no single
organization controls the authority. This contrasts with traditional PKI bridge
CAs, which are single entities.

==== Topology flexibility

The {{trust graph}} shall admit hierarchical, federated, cross-recognized, and
mesh topologies as first-class conformance profiles. An implementation shall not
be required to assume a single-root hierarchy.


=== Multi-party and multi-dimensional attestation

==== Multi-party co-signed artifacts

A {{trusted artifact}} shall carry one or more {{co-signature,co-signatures}},
each from a different signer, attesting the same {{canonical payload}}. The
artifact shall be a convergence point of independent attestations rather than a
single-authority assertion.

==== Multi-dimensional reality attestation

Time, location, and environment shall be cryptographic {{trust dimension,trust
dimensions}}, each backed by its own trust tree and each producing its own
{{dimension attestation}}. These dimensions shall not be carried as unsigned
metadata.

==== Cross-domain trust fusion

{{co-signature,co-signatures}} from independent {{trust chain,trust chains}} or
independent {{root trust authority,root trust authorities}} shall be carried on
the same {{trusted artifact}}. Cross-domain trust recognition shall occur at the
artifact level, not solely at the {{trust authority}} level.


=== Scope and binding

==== Live scope conditions

A {{scope}} shall include {{scope condition,scope conditions}} — executable
predicates evaluated at verification time against the content and context of the
artifact. An artifact signed by a key whose scope conditions are not met shall
fail verification, regardless of cryptographic signature validity.

==== Revocation propagation

Revocation of a {{trust authority}} state shall propagate to every {{trusted
artifact}} transitively bound to that state, across all {{trust
dimension,trust dimensions}} and all {{trust chain,trust chains}}. A revoked
authority's prior signatures shall not remain valid.

==== Challenge-response authenticity

A verifier shall be able to challenge a device for a fresh {{trusted artifact}}
bound to a nonce, such that a static copy of a prior artifact cannot satisfy the
challenge. Authenticity shall be established by the ability to produce a
timely, nonce-bound response, not by possession of a static credential.


=== Transparency and time

==== Mandatory transparency

Inclusion of every {{end certificate}} and every {{trusted artifact}} in a
{{transparency log}} shall be a verification requirement. Artifacts not provably
included in a recognized log shall be downgraded or rejected.

==== Multi-log attestation

A {{federated trust authority}} shall require inclusion of its artifacts in at
least M of K independent {{transparency log,transparency logs}}, such that no
single log operator controls the record.

==== External time anchoring

The time of signing shall be established by an independent {{time key}} or by
anchoring to an external, irrefutable time source. The signer's self-asserted
timestamp shall not be the sole evidence of when an artifact was signed.


=== Trust assessment

==== Graduated trust

The {{classification label}} of a {{trusted artifact}} shall reflect the depth and
breadth of its {{dimensional coverage}}, not merely the binary validity of its
primary signature. A verifier shall be able to apply risk-based policy to the
classification label.
